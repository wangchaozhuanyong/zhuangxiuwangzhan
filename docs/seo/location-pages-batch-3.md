<p>Damansara covers several adjoining neighbourhoods — Damansara Utama, Damansara Jaya, SS20 to SS22 and the streets around them — and most of the housing dates from the 1980s and 1990s. Terraces and semi-detached homes dominate, with low-rise apartments filling the gaps and newer high-rises appearing along the main roads. Because the area matured early, the housing stock is now at the age where systems rather than surfaces decide the budget.</p>
<p><strong>What tends to come up in Damansara</strong></p>
<p>In the terraces and semi-detached homes, the recurring findings are wiring sized for a fraction of today's load, plumbing repaired in sections over the years, roofs that have outlived their intended life, and bathrooms whose waterproofing has been patched more than once. Extensions are common — rear, side, and sometimes a second storey — and those need local authority submission with drawings. Where a house has changed hands, earlier alterations may not have been documented, so establishing what is original is part of the first visit. In the apartments, the work is usually kitchen and bathroom renovation plus storage, within management rules.</p>
<p><strong>Access and site conditions</strong></p>
<p>The commercial cores around SS2 and Damansara Utama are busy through the day, and the residential lanes are narrow with cars parked on both sides, so material delivery has to be scheduled. For apartments, check the lift situation and the management's working-hour rules before the programme is set, because both constrain how fast the work can move.</p>
<p><strong>Typical scope</strong></p>
<p>For a terrace or semi-detached home: full rewiring, re-piping, bathroom and kitchen rebuilding, roof repair, and often a rear or side extension. For an apartment: kitchen and bathroom renovation, wardrobes, flooring and lighting. In both, the electrical capacity check belongs at the start rather than the end, because it determines how many air-conditioning units and appliances the house can actually support.</p>
<p><strong>Quotation and handover</strong></p>

<p>Kota Damansara is a newer township compared with the older parts of Petaling Jaya, developed mainly from the 2000s onward. The housing mix is weighted towards condominiums and serviced apartments, with shops and offices threaded through the same blocks. Because the buildings are relatively young, the work here rarely involves replacing failed systems; it is about making the layout fit how people actually live, and about adding storage and services the original design did not allow for.</p>
<p><strong>What tends to come up in Kota Damansara</strong></p>
<p>The recurring requests are kitchens that were specified for light cooking and need more power and better extraction, bathrooms that need better ventilation, storage that the compact layout never provided, and lighting changes for units with strong afternoon sun. Electrical additions are common, and newer buildings often have tighter circuit allocation, so adding several air-conditioning units or an induction hob without checking capacity is how breakers start tripping. Water pressure on upper floors is another item worth checking before specifying rain showers or instant heaters.</p>
<p><strong>Management and access</strong></p>
<p>Most blocks are managed by a JMB or MC with a formal application, deposit, contractor registration and fixed working hours, and many require lift lobby and corridor protection before materials arrive. Loading is by booked service lift. Because a high proportion of units here are rented, some managements require the owner's written consent before a tenant renovates — worth confirming early if the unit is not owner-occupied. Parking for contractors is usually limited, which affects how much material can be brought per trip.</p>
<p><strong>Typical scope</strong></p>
<p>For a condominium or serviced apartment: kitchen and bathroom renovation, built-in wardrobes and cabinets, flooring, lighting and electrical additions, and air-conditioning relocation. For the older terraces on the edge of the township: rewiring, kitchen and bathroom work, and ceiling and painting. In both, ventilation and electrical capacity are the two items most often taken for granted and most often the reason a plan has to be revised.</p>
<p><strong>Quotation and handover</strong></p>
<p>The quotation separates confirmed work from provisional items, and states what the management office requires from us and from you. At handover we test every outlet, run the taps and check the joints, confirm the air-conditioning drains away rather than into the ceiling, and record defects with a return date. We also confirm the deposit refund steps, which usually depend on a management inspection.</p>
